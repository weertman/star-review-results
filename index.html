The experiment

Each Thursday, Gray and Kendra poke sea stars in the lab's camera tanks for up to about five minutes per star, so the stars aren't over-stressed. With the Poke Trial Logger they record when they enter, the first poke, the first movement, when the star moves away, and when they leave. As they enter, the poker also calls how the star seemed before poking: quiescent, alert or moving. Not every star wakes up and moves away in that time.

That live call is quick and made by someone about to poke, so it may not be reliable. To check it, annotators make the same call blind, usually on the Monday after: they watch the five minutes before the poker entered, sped up 20× into a 15-second loop and cropped to the one tank, on the Star Review site. Each annotator sees every clip twice in a shuffled order, at least five clips apart, must watch a full loop before answering, and can only step back a few clips. They also say whether the star was eating. They never see each other's answers, the live call, or what the star did when poked.

The study runs a round like this every week for eight weeks, with a new star in each container each week, so no star is tested twice on this assay; within its poking day a star is poked several times. This page pools every week so far, and updates as rounds and answers are added. It asks: do annotators agree on what they see, does the state they see predict whether the star responds to poking, and does it change how fast?

Does the state change how fast stars respond?

Statistical summary

The page's main questions as tests, pooling every week, to help tell patterns that are likely real from ones a dataset this size could show by chance. Each row gives an estimate, a 95% interval and, where it applies, a p-value, with a plain reading.

Tests

Intervals are bootstrap percentiles over clips; p-values are two-sided (Fisher's exact test for differences in rates, permutation tests otherwise) and not adjusted for running many tests. AUC is the chance that a clip whose star moved away got a more active call than one whose star didn't: 0.5 means no link, 1 a perfect one.

Reading these results

  • Small samples. Each week adds one new star per container. Until several weeks are in, some calls (such as quiescent from the group) rest on only a few clips, so intervals are wide.
  • Repeated pokes. Each star is poked several times on its day, so its clips aren't independent; the true uncertainty is, if anything, larger than shown. Stars from different weeks are different animals.
  • Many tests. With this many rows, about one in twenty can pass p < 0.05 by chance. Trust patterns that repeat across annotators and across weeks.
  • The tank. If one tank's stars move away more and also get more active calls, the tank alone could make the state look predictive. The within-tank rows check for that.
  • Still collecting. Annotators who haven't finished make their own rows, and the group's, less certain. Each week adds clips and narrows the intervals.

How these numbers are worked out

Cohen's κ
Agreement between two sets of answers after taking away what chance alone would give: 0 is chance, 1 is perfect. A rough reading: up to 0.2 slight, 0.4 fair, 0.6 moderate, 0.8 substantial, above that almost perfect.
Krippendorff's α
The same idea across all annotators at once. It allows for clips that some annotators haven't reached yet.
Which answers count
Agreement uses each annotator's answer from their first viewing of a clip; the second viewing measures repeat consistency. Can't tell is left out of agreement but shown in the answer mix. Answers given before the eating question was added have no eating answer.
Group call
The state most annotators chose on first viewing. A tie for the top answer is Split.
Outcome
From the poke log: moved away when a movement-away time was logged; moved, didn't leave when only a first movement was logged; otherwise no movement.
Clip numbers
Clip numbers (W1-C01 is week 1's first clip) were made for this page and are not the clip names on the review site. Stars are named by week and container (W1 starbot A). This page shows live calls and outcomes, so keep it from anyone still annotating the round.
